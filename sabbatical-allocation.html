Sabbatical allocation worksheet

Distributes a fixed pool of sabbatical seats across colleges in proportion to eligible faculty, using the Hamilton (largest-remainder) method. Every number below can be checked by hand from the inputs.

Inputs

Seats available = % of all eligible faculty, to a whole number.

How the calculation works
Seat pool
Total eligible faculty across all colleges × the percentage, rounded by the rule chosen above.
Exact quota
For each college taking part in a round: seats in the pool × (that college's eligible faculty ÷ eligible faculty of all colleges taking part).
Whole seats and extra seats (Hamilton method)
Each college first receives the whole-number part of its quota. The seats left over, which always number fewer than the colleges taking part, go one each to the colleges with the largest fractional parts. Fractional parts are compared exactly, not as rounded decimals. If two colleges are tied at the cutoff, the seat goes to the one with more eligible faculty; if still tied, alphabetical order by name.
Matching seats to applicants
A college cannot use more seats than it has applicants. Seats beyond a college's applicants are returned to the pool. A college whose applicants are all funded is settled.
Round 1
Every college takes part, including colleges with no applicants. This fixes each college's baseline entitlement from headcount alone, so the same headcounts always produce the same round-1 quotas regardless of who applied.
Later rounds
Returned seats are divided, by the same method, among only the colleges that still have unfunded applicants. Settled colleges do not take part. Because every seat in these rounds goes to a college that can use it, the process ends within a number of rounds no greater than the number of colleges plus one.
Ending
The process stops when the pool is empty or when every college is settled. In the second case the seats left over cannot be placed and are reported as unallocated.
