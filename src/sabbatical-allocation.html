How the allocation is calculated

This page carries out the procedure below. Every number it produces appears in the tables and in the step-by-step record, so each step can be checked by hand from the inputs.

  1. Count the seat pool. Add up the eligible faculty of all colleges. Multiply by the percentage set above (12% unless changed). Round to a whole number by the rule chosen above (rounded up unless changed; "rounded to nearest" rounds an exact half up). The percentage may have up to four decimal places, and the product is worked out exactly, not in rounded decimals. Both the exact product and the rounded result are shown under the inputs.
  2. Round 1: divide the pool among all colleges in proportion to eligible faculty. Every college takes part, including colleges with no applicants. Each college's exact quota is the pool × (its eligible faculty ÷ the eligible faculty of all colleges taking part). Quotas are usually not whole numbers.
  3. Give each college the whole-number part of its quota. These whole seats add up to less than the pool unless every quota happens to be a whole number.
  4. Give the seats left over, one each, to the colleges with the largest fractional parts. This is the Hamilton, or largest-remainder, method. Fractional parts are compared exactly, not as rounded decimals. If colleges are tied at the cutoff, the seat goes to the one with the larger carry forward from previous years (step 8). If their carry forwards are equal, the rule chosen under the inputs decides: the college with more eligible faculty; the college listed higher in the input list, then the one with more eligible faculty; or the college listed higher with nothing after that. If every step of the chosen rule leaves the colleges equal (under the eligible-faculty rule, colleges with the same fractional part, the same carry forward, and the same eligible faculty), the tie is unresolved. The page then marks it as unresolved, shows the seat provisionally with the college listed higher, and asks the committee to decide, for example by switching to a list-order rule and arranging the list. Two colleges never share a position, so a rule that includes list order always decides. Any tie that decides a seat is reported in the round narrative, in the carry-forward report, and in the record.
  5. Match each college's allocation to its applicants. A college cannot use more seats than it has applicants still waiting. Seats it can use are awarded. Seats beyond its applicants are returned to the pool. A college whose applicants are all funded, or that has none, is settled.
  6. Later rounds: divide the returned seats among the colleges that still have unfunded applicants. Steps 2 to 5 are repeated with the returned seats as the pool and only the unsettled colleges taking part, again in proportion to their eligible faculty. Settled colleges do not take part.
  7. Stop. The process ends when the pool is empty or when every college is settled. If seats remain when every college is settled, they are reported as unallocated. Each round either places every seat or settles at least one more college, so the process always ends within a number of rounds no greater than the number of colleges plus one.
  8. Record the carry forward for next year. A college that lost a tie at the cutoff and ends the year with an unfunded applicant adds the fractional part it lost to its carry forward. A college that won a tie starts next year with a carry forward of zero. Every other college's carry forward is unchanged. The values to enter next year are listed under "Carry forward for next year", and can be saved to a file from there.

Why round 1 includes colleges with no applicants

Round 1 establishes each college's entitlement from headcount alone, so the same headcounts always give the same baseline regardless of who applied. A college with no applicants is treated like any other college that cannot use all of its seats: its allocation is returned to the pool and divided among the colleges that can use it.

Why settled colleges leave later rounds

If settled colleges kept taking part, a returned seat could be handed to a college that cannot use it, returned again, and handed to it again without end, while a college with an applicant waiting went without. Restricting later rounds to unsettled colleges sends every returned seat to a college that can use it and guarantees the process ends.

Why there is a carry forward

When two colleges have exactly the same fractional part, the rule that decides the seat has nothing to do with need. The carry forward records the fraction the losing college was denied, so that the next time it is in a tie the seat goes to it. It persists from year to year until the college wins a tie. It is used for nothing else: it does not change quotas, whole seats, or the ranking of fractional parts that are not tied. Within a year, a college that has won a tie is treated as having no carry forward in later rounds.

Files

"Save inputs to file" writes a small text file in JSON format holding everything entered on this page: the seat percentage, the rounding rule, the tie rule, and the colleges in list order with their eligible faculty, applicants, and carry forward. "Load inputs from file" reads such a file back. "Save next year's starting inputs to file", in the carry-forward report, writes the same kind of file with next year's carry forwards filled in and the counts cleared. The results CSV is for spreadsheets and cannot be loaded.

A file can also be written by hand or produced from a spreadsheet, as long as it has this shape:

{
  "format": "sabbatical-allocation-inputs",
  "version": 3,
  "percent": 12,
  "rounding": "up",
  "tieMethod": "eligible",
  "colleges": [
    { "name": "Humanities", "eligible": 39, "applicants": 13, "carry": 0 },
    { "name": "Library",    "eligible": 18, "applicants": 2,  "carry": 0.333 }
  ]
}

Only the colleges list is required, and within each entry only name, eligible, and applicants. A missing carry is taken as 0. A missing percent, rounding (up, nearest, or down), or tieMethod (eligible, list, or list-only) takes the default shown on the page. Names must be unique, counts must be whole numbers, and the order of the list is the order the page shows. Files written by earlier versions of this page load as well.

What to check

The round tables show, for every college in every round, its exact quota, whole seats, fractional part, rank, extra seat, allocation, seats funded, and seats returned. The carry-forward report lists every tie, who won and lost it and by which rule, and the value each college carries into next year. The step-by-step record at the end of the results writes out every division and every seat movement in order, with a checksum for each round and for the whole allocation. The CSV download contains the same tables.
